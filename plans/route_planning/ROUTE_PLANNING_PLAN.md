## Decisions

D-1. Status rule (FR-16). `service/fact_status.py` holds `FactStatus`, a `StrEnum` of `unverified`, `confirmed`, `disputed` and `outdated`; `FactVoteRecord`, a `Protocol` with the read-only members `id: int`, `verdict: VoteVerdict`, `is_cast_with_account: bool`, `account_id: int | None`, `voter_hash: bytes | None` and `cast_at: OffsetInstant`, which `OsmVoteSnapshot` of F-22 already meets; the frozen dataclass `FactStatusResult(status: FactStatus, confirmations: float, denials: float, last_confirmed_on: date | None)`; and `resolve_fact_status(votes: Iterable[FactVoteRecord], is_removed_from_osm: bool) -> FactStatusResult`. The person of a vote is its `account_id`, else its `voter_hash`, else - a vote of a deleted account - the vote itself by `id`. The latest vote of a person and the five persons who voted most recently are ordered by `cast_at.instant` and then by `id`, so of two equal instants the later stored vote counts as the later one. Weights are the constants `ACCOUNT_VOTE_WEIGHT = 1.0` and `ANONYMOUS_VOTE_WEIGHT = 0.5`, the window `STATUS_PERSON_COUNT = 5` and the threshold `STATUS_WEIGHT_THRESHOLD = 2.0`; the order of M4 decides the status, and `is_removed_from_osm` makes it outdated while the sums are still returned, because the importer compares them (F-21). `last_confirmed_on` is the calendar day in Europe/Warsaw of the latest confirmation among all votes of the fact, as scenario 2 of the shape of `community_facts` shows it. Multiples of 0.5 are exact in a float, so the sums need no decimal type. The interface was ruled into this initiative by Rafał in the PRD amendment; the order of equal instants and the day over all votes are an agent decision at C:40, without asking: the identity of `vote` grows with every insert, and M4 says older votes stay in the history and only stop counting toward the status, not toward the day of the latest confirmation.

D-2. Calendar day. `common_time.py` gets `build_business_day(instant: datetime) -> date`, the day of an aware instant in the zone of `BUSINESS_TIMEZONE`, used by D-1 and by `osm_copy_date`. Agent decision at C:40, without asking: one place for the zone of M10 next to `fetch_business_now` (F-28).

D-3. Engine and snapshot. `fetch_api_engine` of F-5 is used as delivered; when `data/engine.py` does not hold it when step S-4 starts, this plan adds it exactly as D-2 of the plan of `accounts` names it. `data/engine.py` gets `fetch_read_only_snapshot(engine: Engine) -> Iterator[Connection]`, a context manager that opens a connection with `isolation_level` `REPEATABLE READ` and `postgresql_readonly` true, begins a transaction and rolls it back at the end, and `apply_statement_timeout(connection: Connection, milliseconds: int) -> None`, which runs `SELECT set_config('statement_timeout', :timeout, true)` with the value as a bound parameter. Every read of one route request runs in one such snapshot (F-6). Agent decision at C:40, without asking: the shared helper of F-4 and the transaction of D-8 of the architecture.

D-4. Copy in use and served copy (FR-8). `resolve_route` reads the copy in use with `fetch_current_osm_copy` in its snapshot; no copy ends the request with `RoutingUnavailableError`. It compares `math.floor(state_at.instant.timestamp())` with `fetch_served_copy_instant()`; a difference or a failed call ends the request with `RoutingUnavailableError`. Agent decision at C:40, without asking: F-8 and F-9 give whole seconds on one side and a float on the other, and the floor is what `stat` reports and what names the directory (F-7).

D-5. Boundary of Kraków (FR-7, FR-15). The import writes the boundary of F-11 as WKB of a polygon or multipolygon in longitude and latitude to `krakow_boundary.wkb` in the directory of its copy, before the manifest, and adds the name to `ROUTING_FILE_NAMES`, so the manifest covers it; this is the need handed to `osm_importer` in S-1. `data/routing_data.py` gets the constant `ROUTING_BOUNDARY_NAME = "krakow_boundary.wkb"`, `build_routing_copy_directory(root: Path, state_at: datetime) -> Path`, which gives `root / "copies" / str(math.floor(state_at.timestamp()))` for the writer and the reader alike, and `fetch_osm_boundary(directory: Path) -> bytes`, which reads the manifest through `fetch_osm_routing_manifest`, refuses with `RoutingDataError` a manifest whose `state_at` differs from the copy or that does not list the boundary, and returns the bytes of the file. `service/route_boundary.py` holds the boundary as a cache of the process keyed by the instant of the copy, rebuilt under a `threading.Lock` when the instant changes and never filled from a failed read, and `resolve_points_outside_krakow(state_at: datetime, start: RoutePoint, destination: RoutePoint) -> tuple[str, ...]`, which returns `start`, `destination`, both in that order or none, a point on the boundary counting as inside. A missing or refused boundary ends the request with `RoutingUnavailableError`, because without it the rule of M2 cannot be applied and no point is guessed inside or outside. The check runs after the copy in use is read and before the served copy is compared, so a point outside is refused whatever the routing service does. Where the boundary lives was decided by Rafał in phase B, against a table in the database; the rest is an agent decision at C:40, without asking: the manifest already guards the files of a copy, and the order keeps the refusal a property of the request.

D-6. Error codes (FR-7, FR-10). `service/route_planning.py` holds `RoutingUnavailableError` and `PointOutsideKrakowError(points: tuple[str, ...])`. The input layer answers the first with 503 `routing_unavailable` and the second with 422 `point_outside_krakow` and the field `points`, for example `{ "error": { "code": "point_outside_krakow", "points": ["start"] } }`; `build_error_response` gets the parameter `points: Sequence[str] = ()`, written only for that code, as `fields` is only for `invalid_request`. The code, the status and the field were decided by Rafał in phase B, against two codes of their own.

D-7. Configuration. `config/settings.py` gets the required entries `ROUTING_SERVICE_URL: str`, an `http` or `https` address, and `ROUTING_DATA_DIR: Path`, an absolute path, both in `.env.local` of `ENVIRONMENT_ENTRY_FILES` and in `.env.local.example`; `config/config.py` exposes both; `docs/standards/standard_config.md`, section Environment entries, describes both. Agent decision at C:40, without asking: both processes now read both (F-24, D-5), and a required entry fails at the start instead of in a route.

D-8. Calls to the routing service. `data/valhalla.py` is the one call site (`docs/standards/standard_architecture.md`, Calls to external systems): `build_valhalla_client() -> httpx.Client`, kept by `functools.cache`, with `base_url` `ROUTING_SERVICE_URL`, the timeout `VALHALLA_CALL_TIMEOUT_SECONDS = 2.0`, `trust_env` false and `follow_redirects` false; `fetch_served_copy_instant() -> int` reading `tileset_last_modified` of `GET /status`; `fetch_valhalla_route(body: Mapping[str, object]) -> ValhallaRoute` for `POST /route`, with `ValhallaRoute(shape: str, length_km: float)` from the first leg; `fetch_valhalla_trace(body: Mapping[str, object]) -> ValhallaTrace` for `POST /trace_attributes`, with `ValhallaTrace(shape: str, edges: tuple[ValhallaEdge, ...])` and `ValhallaEdge(way_id: int, begin_node_id: int, end_node_id: int, begin_shape_index: int, end_shape_index: int)` read from `way_id`, `node_id`, `end_node.node_id`, `begin_shape_index` and `end_shape_index` (F-16). An answer with `error_code` 442 raises `RoutingServiceNoRouteError`, 443 `RoutingServiceTraceError`, and every other failure, a timeout and a malformed body `RoutingServiceUnavailableError`; all three derive from `RoutingServiceError`. The module retries nothing; the repetitions of D-12 and D-13 are rules. No answer is cached, because each one belongs to its request and `/status` has to follow a restart (D-6 of the architecture); the calls run in the thread pool, because the endpoint is a plain `def` (D-2 of the architecture). Agent decision at C:40, without asking: D-10 of the Valhalla plan, F-25 and the standard.

D-9. Walking request (FR-1). `service/route_requests.py` builds the bodies: the two locations; `costing` `pedestrian` with the options of D-4 of the Valhalla plan in the constant `PEDESTRIAN_COSTING_OPTIONS`; `exclude_locations` with `radius` 0 and `minimum_reachability` 0, a barrier of a stretch at the middle of the longest pair of consecutive nodes of the stretch with `node_snap_tolerance` 0, one location for every stretch of a way whose way fact is avoided, and a barrier of a node at the node with `node_snap_tolerance` 1; `exclude_polygons` with the polygon of 32 vertices circumscribed about the circle of each avoided geozone, computed in `EPSG:2180`, except a geozone whose circle holds the start or the destination. The corridor is the rectangle around the straight line between the two points, each side `max(1000, length / 4)` metres wide, computed in `EPSG:2180` with the constants of `service/osm_geometry.py`. The trace body is `encoded_polyline` of the route, `shape_match` `edge_walk` or `walk_or_snap` and the same costing. The polyline of precision 6 is decoded by `build_polyline_points(encoded: str) -> tuple[tuple[float, float], ...]` in that module. The constants `CORRIDOR_MIN_HALF_WIDTH_M = 1000`, `GEOZONE_POLYGON_VERTICES = 32`, `REPORT_STRETCH_DISTANCE_M = 15`, `KERB_CONTRADICTION_DISTANCE_M = 5` and `AMENITY_DISTANCE_M = 50` stand next to the rules that use them. What a route avoids is decided by `resolve_avoided_barriers` of the same module: an OpenStreetMap barrier fact of the profile whose status is not outdated and that no contradicting user report with confirmations of at least 2 replaces, a user barrier report of the profile in the status confirmed that lies on a stretch, and a geozone of a type of the profile in a status other than outdated.
