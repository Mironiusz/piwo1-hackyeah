## Decisions

D-1. Routes are computed by a graph of the pedestrian network held in the memory of the one backend process and searched with the shortest path algorithm of scipy over numpy arrays. Decided by the user, answering for the backend person, on 2026-10-03 in phase B, against pgRouting in the database of the demo, which needs the barriers of the profile and the user facts written into the edge query passed as text (F-15) against F-14, and against a Valhalla run by the project, which builds its tiles from a file the import deletes (F-4), reads the tags by its own costing instead of M6 (F-17) and adds a second service to the server. OSRM was not offered, because a new vote could not change the next route without a new preprocessing of the data (F-16), against PRD FR-1. A routing service outside the project is excluded by M2 (F-2). The measurements behind the choice are F-25 and F-26: a route across Kraków in at most 0.49 seconds, ten times under the 5 seconds of the PRD, at 255 MB.

D-2. The route is computed in the rules layer from data the data layer reads, and nothing of the computation reads the database or the network itself, so it is tested without them (F-18, F-19). The rules layer holds the graph, the cost, the segment states, the alternative, the route with the fewest barriers, the segment combination of `plans_finished/osm_barrier_mapping/` D-7 and the contradiction of M2 (F-7). The names of modules and functions are decided with Q-11 of the MVP plan, as `plans_finished/osm_barrier_mapping/` D-14 did for the tag rule (F-8). Agent decision at C:40, without asking: it follows from F-18 and F-19.

D-3. The graph is a cache of the copy in use under F-18, invalidated by comparison with the source. It is built when the backend process starts, from the network the import stored (D-11). Every route request reads, in one read-only transaction, the instant of the copy in use (F-5) together with the facts and geozones it needs (D-12); when that instant differs from the instant the graph was built from, the graph is rebuilt from the same transaction before the route is computed. The rebuild holds a lock inside the process, so concurrent requests wait for it instead of building twice. Ways, facts and the date of one route therefore always come from one copy (PRD FR-1), and a new vote, report or geozone counts in the next route without a restart or an import, because facts are read for every request. When the graph cannot be built, the request gets the plain message of MVP PRD FR-17 (D-13). Agent decision at C:40, without asking: it follows from PRD FR-1, F-4, F-5, F-12 and F-18.

D-4. A way is part of the pedestrian network when its `highway` is `footway`, `path`, `pedestrian`, `steps`, `living_street`, `track`, `bridleway`, `corridor`, `road`, `platform`, `elevator` or one of the ways for motor traffic of M6 - `trunk`, `primary`, `secondary`, `tertiary`, their `_link` ways, `unclassified`, `residential` and `service` - or when it is `highway=cycleway` with `foot` of `yes`, `designated`, `permissive`, `destination`, `delivery` or `customers`; and when none of these holds: `foot` is `no`, `private` or `use_sidepath`; the way is `motorroad=yes`; `access` is `no` or `private` and `foot` is none of the values above; the way is for motor traffic and `sidewalk` or `sidewalk:both` is `separate`. Any other `highway` value, `motorway`, `motorway_link`, `construction` and `proposed` among them, is outside the network. The network of the copy of 2026-10-02 holds 130 864 ways, and each excluding rule removes the ways counted in F-24. Excluding the ways for motor traffic with a separately mapped sidewalk and the ways `highway=cycleway` without a permission for pedestrians was decided by the user on 2026-10-03 in phase B, against keeping them; the other excluding values follow from the rule of M2 that the user decided in `ROUTING_ENGINE_SHAPE.md`, question 5, and the including values were proposed by the agent from F-24 and common OpenStreetMap tagging. The import stores only the ways of the network, and the graph uses every stored way, so the list lives in one place: a constant of the third configuration layer next to the tag rule of the import (F-8, F-20). Agent decision at C:40, without asking, for the place of the list: a second copy in the route would be a second place for one rule.

D-5. A node of the graph is a node that ends a stored way of the network or belongs to two or more of them. A stretch is the part of one way between two consecutive nodes of the graph; it keeps the OpenStreetMap identity of its way, the positions of its first and last node in that way, its geometry and its length in metres. The point facts and kerb points of every node of a stretch, its two end nodes included, count for that stretch under D-7 of `plans_finished/osm_barrier_mapping/` (F-7). A segment of a route is a stretch, or the part of the stretch where the route joins the network (D-9), plus the two stretches to the network. Agent decision at C:40, without asking: D-7 there leaves the segment to the route, and a stretch between junctions is the smallest piece a route can take or leave.

D-6. The weight of a stretch is its length in metres plus 1 000 000 when the stretch carries a barrier the route avoids under M2: a barrier from the profile known from OpenStreetMap that is not outdated and not replaced by a contradicting report that reached 2.0, a user barrier from the profile that prevails, or a geozone of a type in the profile that is unverified, confirmed or disputed and covers the stretch (D-8). An unverified or disputed user barrier from the profile that OpenStreetMap does not contradict adds nothing, so the route keeps it and its segment is red (D-7). The shortest path under these weights is the route with the fewest stretches carrying an avoided barrier and, among those, the shortest one, which is the route of MVP PRD FR-2 when it reaches no barrier and the route of FR-4 otherwise; a total weight of 1 000 000 or more gives the plain statement of FR-4 and the list of where the barriers are, each barrier fact named once. 1 000 000 m is more than thirty times the longest route measured (F-26). Agent decision at C:40, without asking: it follows from MVP PRD FR-2 and FR-4.

D-7. When the route keeps one or more unverified or disputed barriers from the profile that OpenStreetMap does not contradict, one alternative is computed with the stretches of those barriers weighted like avoided ones. It is proposed when its path differs from the route, with the reason naming each of those barriers it avoids and its status. Agent decision at C:40, without asking: it follows from MVP PRD FR-3 and from the single alternative of the route response in `plans/api_contract/` (F-31).

D-8. A point report lies on the stretch of the network nearest to it, when that stretch is no more than 15 m away; a report farther from every stretch is shown on the map but changes no route and no segment. A kerb report is contradicted by an opposite kerb point of OpenStreetMap on the stretch it lies on and no more than 5 m from it. A report of a barrier of a way is contradicted when the stretch it lies on carries the state absent for that barrier, never absent by default or unknown (M2, F-7). A geozone covers every stretch any part of which lies within its radius. The 15 m and the 5 m were decided by the user on 2026-10-03 in phase B, against 10 m and 25 m, and against 10 m and 15 m; the contradiction by the state absent follows from M2; the cover of a geozone was proposed by the agent. Agent decision at C:40, without asking, for the cover of a geozone: a geozone is a circle (F-9), so the part of a way inside it is what the person marked.

D-9. A route joins the network at the point of the nearest stretch closest to the chosen start or destination, taking only stretches of the largest connected part of the network, so that the start and the destination are always connected. The stretch to the network is a straight segment in the state no data. Its stairs are unknown, so for a profile that avoids stairs the list names the steps among its missing attributes, next to the kerbs, the surface, the incline and the width the profile needs. Decided by the user on 2026-10-03 in phase B for the stairs, against the default of no stairs of M7; the point of joining and the largest connected part are an agent decision at C:40, without asking: they follow from M2, under which the stretch to the network carries what the network does not know, and they keep an isolated piece of the network from leaving a route without a path.

D-10. The amenities from the profile within 50 m of the route are read from the database by their distance to the geometry of the route, with the geometry, the types and the distance as parameters of the query; hidden and outdated ones are left out. Agent decision at C:40, without asking: it follows from M8 and F-14.

D-11. The stored copy keeps, for every way of the pedestrian network, the ordered list of its nodes with the OpenStreetMap identifier and the coordinates of each node, in the same commit as the rest of the copy (F-5). FR-5 of `plans/fact_schema/` does not name it (F-9), the copy lives only in the database (F-4), and no engine can build a network without it. Raised with the db person under U-2 of `plans/dependency_check/` (F-10) and accepted by the user for the db person on 2026-10-03 in phase B, against waiting for the db person; how it is stored and named is for the task `FACT_SCHEMA` of `plans/fact_schema/` (F-11).

D-12. A route request reads, in the transaction of D-3: the instant of the copy in use; the user facts of barrier types that are neither hidden nor outdated, with their statuses derived by the status rule of `plans/fact_schema/`, called and never rewritten; the OpenStreetMap facts that have at least one vote, with their statuses, every other OpenStreetMap fact being unverified and counting until outdated (M4); the geozones of types in the profile that are neither hidden nor outdated; and the amenities of D-10. Every read uses the database timeout that Q-11 sets for the backend, and an expired timeout gives the plain message of MVP PRD FR-17 (F-19). Agent decision at C:40, without asking: it follows from D-3, from F-19 and from the rule of `docs/standards/standard_architecture.md` that a rule called from many places lives in one place.

D-13. Routing stops answering only when the graph cannot be built - no copy in the database, or a failed build - or when a read of D-12 fails or times out. The person then gets the plain message of MVP PRD FR-17 and no guessed route, and the request is never passed to a routing service outside the project. This initiative decides no switch for making routing unreachable in the live demo; that is for the task `DEPLOYMENT` of `plans/demo_environment/` (F-30). Agent decision at C:40, without asking: it follows from MVP PRD FR-17 and M2.

Both pass pip-audit and carry BSD licences (F-27). Agent decision at C:40, without asking: no written pinning rule for runtime dependencies exists, so the form of the existing pins is followed.
